# Layout: deciding a surface before drawing it

SKILL.md states the four principles (purpose, hierarchy, grouping, sizing) and the layout note.
This file shows the reasoning on more cases, and how to tell a decided surface from an undecided one.

## How to find the purpose

- Read the ask for verbs. "See", "check", "track" are glances; "answer", "pick", "pay", "reply" are
  actions. An action outranks a glance: it is why the person opens the surface today.
- Look at the data for what changes. A value that changes hourly is worth a glance; one that never
  changes is a label.
- Ask what they do after looking. If the answer is "open X", the surface is a way into X, and the
  lead is the way in.
- When two questions tie, ask the person. Do not average them into two equal boxes.

## How to pick the lead

The lead is the answer to the top question, in the smallest form that answers it.

| The top question is | The lead is | Its size |
|---|---|---|
| "does anything need me?" with one item | a band: what it is, one action | one row |
| "does anything need me?" with several | a short list, most urgent first | its rows, at most five, the rest behind a count |
| "what is the number?" | the figure | one display line |
| "what is on?" over a span of time | the calendar or timeline for that span | the span's natural width |
| "which one should I choose?" | the pick, with why it wins | one card, wider than the runners-up |

A lead that has nothing to show is removed, not left as an empty box saying "nothing waiting".

## How to group

Ask of any two things: does one explain, summarise, control or belong to the other?

- Yes: they share a section. The summary is the first line, the control sits on the row it
  changes, the detail sits under its item.
- No: they are separate sections. Separate with space and a heading. A border around each section
  is rarely needed; a page of bordered boxes makes every section look equally important.

Cards are for items of the same kind laid side by side (areas, products, days). A section that
holds cards gets no card of its own.

## How to size

- Width comes from what must be compared side by side. Seven days, three plans, two columns of a
  form: that count sets the width. Text that is read, not compared, wants a reading width, not the
  full page: lines under about 80 characters, 60–75 at best.
- Height comes from the busiest real case. Then check the empty case and the longest name.
- Siblings in a row share one height. Unequal siblings mean the content differs: either it should
  (then they are not siblings) or it should not (then even it out).
- Importance is shown by position and by being alone, before it is shown by size.

## Signs a surface was not decided

- Blocks are in the order the data arrived or the order the ask listed them.
- Every block is the same width, or the same box.
- A single number has a box as large as a list.
- The thing to act on is neither first nor marked.
- Colour appears on more than the one thing that needs the person.
- You cannot say in one sentence what a block is for.
- The note would read the same for anyone's dashboard: nothing in it comes from this subject.
- Nothing is memorable, or everything tries to be: no single place took the craft.
- Structure that says nothing: a label over every heading, a chip on every row, numbers on
  things that are not steps, a divider between things that belong together.

Each is fixed by going back to the layout note, not by restyling.

## More worked examples

### A project status page

```
Comes here to:   1. know whether it is on track   2. see what is blocked   3. see what is next
Leads:           the verdict in words ("2 of 3 checks passed, 1 running") with one progress bar.
Together:        blocked items directly under the verdict: they are its reason.
                 "next" is a separate, quieter list.
Sizes:           verdict one line and a bar; blocked as tall as its rows; next capped at five.
Cut:             a chart of velocity: nobody asked how fast.
```

### A comparison of three options

```
Comes here to:   1. see which to choose and why   2. check the others are really worse
Leads:           the pick, with the one reason it wins.
Together:        the runners-up side by side under it, each with the one thing it loses on.
Sizes:           the pick full width; runners-up equal columns; the full field one line at the end.
Cut:             a table of every attribute: it hides the reason in rows.
```

### A page with nothing to act on

```
Comes here to:   1. read this week's notes   2. find an older note
Leads:           this week's notes, open, in reading width.
Together:        older notes as a list under them, by month.
Sizes:           one reading column; no cards.
Cut:             counts, tags and a search box for twelve notes.
```

Not every surface has an action. Then the top question's content leads, and nothing takes the accent.
